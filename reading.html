Reading guide

Into the representation theory of p-adic groups

When I started, it took me several years to figure out a streamlined way into this subject. This page is the route I wish someone had handed me: a geodesic from undergraduate algebra, topology and analysis to the “Langlandsy” world, with as few detours as possible.

You need far fewer books than you think

During your basic training (bachelor’s and master’s) you do not need a shelf of textbooks. Anthony Knapp’s books, freely downloadable from his webpage, are more than enough: together they cover essentially everything you will need, with algebraic topology the only notable omission. There is really not much heavy background needed to get started on representations of p-adic groups.

My own Ph.D. thesis is also written to be read with minimal prerequisites: undergraduate-level algebra and topology are enough to follow it.

00Background

From undergraduate mathematics you need the usual courses in algebra, up to the Galois theory of finite extensions; this is what standard curricula offer. The ambitious student should also know the Galois theory of infinite extensions, together with the basics of profinite groups that come with it. Add a standard first course in topology. There is not much need for analysis, unless you are heading towards representations of Lie groups or automorphic representations.

Everything an enthusiastic student needs, at undergraduate and graduate level, is available for free on Anthony Knapp’s webpage. He is one of the greatest expositors of mathematics.

Route IMod p representations

Begin with the finite group: ideally, the mod-p representations of GL2(𝔽q) or SL2(𝔽q). Since I work on SL2, the route below goes through SL2, and passes through my thesis on the way to the p-adic group.

  1. 1 The finite group

    C. Bonnafé, Representations of SL2(𝔽q)

    Algebra and Applications, Springer; 2nd edition 2026 (1st edition 2011)

    Read Chapter 1 (the structure of SL2(𝔽q)) and §10.1, Simple Modules, in Part IV (modular representations in equal characteristic). The goal is the classification of the irreducible 𝔽p-representations of SL2(𝔽q): there are exactly q of them, described by Steinberg’s tensor product theorem (Theorem 10.1.9 of the 2nd edition). The rest of the book (Deligne–Lusztig theory, ℓ-modular representations) is beautiful, but not needed for this route.

    Springer
  2. 2 Two models of the same weights

    My thesis, Chapter 2: Preliminaries

    A. Das, Hecke actions and a restriction problem for mod-p representations of SL2(F), Ph.D. thesis, HRI / HBNI, 2026

    Once the classification over SL2(𝔽q) is understood, go straight to the preliminary chapter of my thesis.

    • §2.2 develops Carter–Lusztig’s way of modelling the mod-p irreducibles of SL2(𝔽q) from first principles: they sit inside induced representations IndBΓ(χ) and are indexed by pairs (χ, J) (Corollary 2.2.50).
    • §2.3 classifies the simple modules of the finite Hecke algebra EndΓ(IndUΓ1) (Theorem 2.3.9) and shows how the Carter–Lusztig model corresponds to the symmetric-power model you met in Bonnafé (Theorem 2.3.12, Proposition 2.3.17).
    • §2.1 collects the general machinery for locally profinite groups (compact induction, Frobenius reciprocity, Hecke algebras), and §§2.4–2.5 the decompositions of SL2(F) and the spherical Hecke algebra ≅ 𝔽p[τ] (Proposition 2.5.6).
    Thesis PDF
  3. 3 The p-adic group

    My thesis, Chapters 3–4: Hecke eigenvalues and the classification

    For F any non-archimedean local field of residue characteristic p

    Continue with the thesis for the classification of the smooth irreducible mod-p representations of SL2(F), up to supersingularity, without assuming admissibility. The Hecke eigenvalues are the engine:

    • Every irreducible V receives a non-zero map from a compact induction indKGσ of some weight σ.
    • Explicit Iwahori–Hecke computations give finiteness results (Propositions 3.2.12 and 3.2.15): the Hecke-submodule generated by such a map is finite-dimensional, so the spherical Hecke operator τσ has an eigenvalue λ, with no admissibility needed. Hence V is a quotient of ind σ / (τσ − λ) (Proposition 3.2.16).
    • If λ ≠ 0, then V is the trivial character, the Steinberg representation or a principal series; if λ = 0, V is supersingular (Theorem 3.3.8).
    • Supersingulars exist because ind σ is free over the spherical Hecke algebra, so τσ is not surjective (Theorem 3.1.5, Remark 3.1.10, Proposition 3.3.5).

    Chapter 4 then shows that the Borel subgroup controls irreducibility and intertwining (Theorem 4.0.1): the SL2-analogue of Paškūnas’s restriction theorem for GL2(F).

    Thesis PDF
  4. ∥ Read alongside stops 2–3

    Barthel–Livné and Breuil: the GL2 story

    • L. Barthel, R. Livné, Irreducible modular representations of GL2 of a local field, Duke Math. J. 75 (1994): the founding paper, with the four classes for GL2(F).
    • C. Breuil, Sur quelques représentations modulaires et p-adiques de GL2(ℚp): Part I, Compositio Math. 138 (2003), classifies the supersingular representations of GL2(ℚp); Part II, J. Inst. Math. Jussieu 2 (2003), continues the story.

    Read in parallel with the thesis, these give a full picture for comparing the GL2 and SL2 theories. The thesis points out where they diverge, e.g. Remark 3.2.14 (central characters) and Remark 4.1.7 (the Borel restriction).

    Barthel–Livné Breuil I Breuil II
  5. 4 On your own

    Where to go next

    I will not say more. Once the above is understood you can start exploring on your own. Two directions:

    • Constructing supersingular representations for groups of relative semisimple rank 1, via coefficient systems on the Bruhat–Tits tree. The places to look are Paškūnas’s memoir Coefficient systems and supersingular representations of GL2(F) (Mém. SMF 99, 2004) and Breuil–Paškūnas, Towards a modulo p Langlands correspondence for GL2 (Mem. AMS 216, 2012).
    • Higher rank. F. Herzig, The classification of irreducible admissible mod p representations of a p-adic GLn (Invent. Math. 186, 2011), which generalises Barthel–Livné to GLn. A tip: unless you are fluent in linear algebraic groups or group schemes, take Herzig’s general arguments on connected reductive groups and try to prove them yourself for GLn first.
    Paškūnas Breuil–Paškūnas Herzig

Route IIComplex (and mod ℓ) representations

Now the complex, or more generally mod-ℓ with ℓ ≠ p, representations of p-adic groups. I will mostly talk about complex representations.

  1. 1 The general theory

    D. Renard, Representations of p-adic Reductive Groups

    Originally Représentations des groupes réductifs p-adiques, Cours Spécialisés 17, SMF, 2010; English translation and corrected French version 2026

    The most general reference I know. I read the French original in 2023–24; the English translation is new in 2026, released together with a corrected French version, and both are free on the author’s page. Chapters I–IV build the smooth representation theory of totally disconnected groups, Chapter V recalls the structure of p-adic reductive groups, and Chapters VI–VII are the heart: supercuspidal representations, the Bernstein decomposition and centre, and the Langlands classification.

    Same advice as before: read general arguments on reductive groups by specialising them to GLn, unless you are already familiar with linear algebraic groups at the level of T. A. Springer’s Linear Algebraic Groups.

    English (2026) Français (2026)
  2. 2 The arithmetic side

    C. J. Bushnell, G. Henniart, The Local Langlands Conjecture for GL(2)

    Grundlehren der mathematischen Wissenschaften 335, Springer, 2006

    The second, and more popular, direction. Read it in parallel with J. Neukirch’s Class Field Theory (Grundlehren 280, Springer, 1986), whose background is already built by Chapters VI and VII of Knapp’s Advanced Algebra.

    In fact, local class field theory is the only prerequisite for Bushnell–Henniart besides what people call “mathematical maturity”. Logically, an advanced undergraduate could read it, though they may find it hard going.

    Bushnell–Henniart Neukirch
  3. 3 Further

    C. J. Bushnell, P. C. Kutzko, The Admissible Dual of GL(N) via Compact Open Subgroups

    Annals of Mathematics Studies 129, Princeton University Press, 1993

    Bushnell–Henniart is a good entry to the more advanced theory of types, for which Bushnell–Kutzko is the standard first resource.

    Princeton
